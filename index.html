The city never stops. It just changes audience.

After midnight, Accra belongs to the stragglers, the night watchmen, the young couples counting cedis for a taxi. The generators hum and highlife thins out from a spot you cannot see. A food seller's bulb throws warm orange light across the wet tar and no one notices because everyone is somewhere else in their mind.

There is a particular loneliness to being awake in a city where, by daylight, nobody is ever left alone. Not painful loneliness — the kind that makes you feel specific, real, briefly unlike everyone you know.

— 01

Solitude
as signal

By day this city will not let you be alone: someone is always calling you over, selling you something, asking after your mother. The quiet between yourself and others is where you hear what you actually think.

— 02

Reflection
as truth

A sign in a puddle doesn't lie the way it does on the wall. The reflection strips away the slogan and gives you only light — only colour — only shape.

— 03

Waiting
as practice

Every missed trotro is a rehearsal for the larger delays. This city teaches patience: nothing here leaves until it is full. There is always a next one, and the question is what you do with the interval.
